Problem and product

  • Traditional church giving was concentrated around weekly services and physical collection methods.
  • Weather, travel, holidays, and attendance changes could interrupt donations.
  • Recurring and mobile giving allow donors to contribute at any time.
  • The platform expanded into a wider set of church-management and engagement tools.

Founder and origin

  • Dean Sweetman observed that members had limited opportunities to give outside weekly gatherings.
  • He wanted a simpler way to support recurring donations and avoid revenue volatility caused by missed services.
  • His son Barnabas spent roughly 12 months building the early application.
  • The first rollout occurred at Dean's Atlanta congregation in 2013.

Behind the scenes: build and launch

  • The early product focused on mobile and digital giving.
  • A real congregation served as the initial test environment.
  • The company used payment infrastructure rather than attempting to build the full payments layer from scratch.
  • The profile later described adoption by more than 37,000 churches.

Behind the scenes: costs and operations

  • The cited profile did not expose a complete founding-cost breakdown.
  • Payment processing, compliance, reliability, and donor trust are central operating requirements.
  • The June 2024 profile estimated approximately 180 employees.
  • Acquisitions and partnerships expanded the platform beyond its initial single-product scope.

Monetization and pricing

  • A historical source claim described an effective transaction rate of approximately 0.92% with no monthly subscription for the giving product.
  • The wider product suite may use additional pricing structures.
  • Transaction rates and plan terms should be checked on the official website because payment pricing changes over time.

Growth and customer acquisition

  • Organic search and paid search helped churches find digital-giving solutions.
  • Referrals, church conferences, and denominational relationships created trust in a specialized market.
  • Acquisitions expanded product capability and customer reach.
  • Partnerships and free access for some new church planters supported distribution.
  • A strong vertical focus made messaging and industry partnerships more specific.

Key business lessons

  • Vertical-market trust can be built through authentic domain experience and in-community launch partners.
  • A transaction-based model can align revenue with customer success and payment volume.
  • Starting with one high-frequency workflow can become an entry point into a broader operating platform.
